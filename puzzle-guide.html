Learn the Chessboard Squares

Every square on a chessboard has a name. The letters a–h identify the columns, while the numbers 1–8 identify the rows.

For example, the square where the letter e and number 4 meet is called e4.

♙ PAWN MOVES

How a Pawn Moves

LESSON 3

How a Pawn Moves

When naming a pawn, the pawn does not have a specific letter to name it.

Instead, it makes use of the square name to show where it moves.

For example, in this particular image, the pawn is initially at g2.

So, if it moves twice, we simply say:

g4

This means that the pawn moved to the g4 square.

We do not write anything else. We simply write g4.

⚔️ PAWN CAPTURES

How Pawn Captures Are Written

LESSON 4

How to Write a Pawn Capture

In order to write the chess notation for how a pawn captures, let us study their positions first.

This white pawn is initially at e4, and this black pawn is initially at d5.

So, for the white pawn to capture the black pawn, the chess notation will be:

exd5

This means the pawn on e4 captured the pawn on d5.

So, we simply write exd5.

♟️ CHESS PIECES

Pieces and Their Names

LESSON 5

Pieces and Their Names

We have the king, queen, knight, bishop, and rook. They are represented by capital letters.

R Rook
K King
Q Queen
N Knight
B Bishop

The pawn does not have a letter, so we simply use the square it is moving to.

In this particular image, the pawn is going to b5, so we write:

b5

For the rook, since it is represented by R, we write:

Rc7

For the king, we write:

Ke5

For the queen, we write:

Qg2

For the knight, we write:

Ng6

For the bishop, we write:

Be5
⚔️ PIECE CAPTURES

How Pieces Capture

LESSON 6

How to Write a Piece Capture

In order to write the chess notation for a piece capture, we use x to represent the capture.

For this particular image, the bishop is capturing the black knight.

The bishop is represented by B, and the knight is on f5.

So, the chess notation is:

Bxf5

This means that the bishop captures the piece on f5.

For the black knight, if it wants to capture the queen on b5, the chess notation is:

Nxb5

This means that the knight captures the piece on b5.

So, for pieces, we write the piece's capital letter, followed by x, and then the square where the capture takes place.

⚡ CHECK

How to Write Checks

LESSON 7

The Plus Symbol Means Check

Checks are written using the plus symbol (+).

For example, if the rook moves to f1 and checks the king, the chess notation is:

Rf1+

This means that the rook moved to the f1 square and checked the king.

♛ CHECKMATE

How to Write Checkmate

LESSON 8

The # Symbol Means Checkmate

Checkmate is written using the # symbol.

For this particular example, the rook is moving to the g1 square and checkmating the king.

So, the chess notation is:

Rg1#

The # represents checkmate.

That is how we write checkmate in chess notation.

👑 PROMOTION

How to Write Promotion

LESSON 9

Using the Equals Symbol for Promotion

To write a promotion in chess, we use the = symbol.

For this particular example, the pawn on e7 is moving to e8.

Since the pawn is promoting to a queen, we write:

e8=Q

This means the pawn moved to e8 and promoted to a queen.
